Galaxy Searchlight · Dublin

The Milky Way in three dimensions, with the Sun 26,700 light-years from its centre. Your horizon is a plane through the Sun: here it is a see-through sheet with a compass ring, and an arrow pointing straight up. Where it cuts the galaxy's disc, the half above is lit as a beam. As the Earth turns, the sheet tilts and the beam sweeps round.

  • Your horizon, with N, E, S and W round the Sun, and rings 5,000, 10,000, 20,000 and 40,000 light-years out
  • Overhead: straight up from where you stand
  • The beam: directions in the disc above your horizon, brighter the higher they stand. Stars below your horizon are dimmed
  • Where your horizon cuts the disc, labelled with the compass point at each end
  • The highest direction in the disc right now
  • The Sun: you are here
  • Globular cluster, bright when above your horizon. They swarm round the centre, above and below the disc, which is how Harlow Shapley found where it was in 1918
  • Open cluster: young, and close to us in the disc
  • Nebula (bright, planetary or supernova remnant)

Drag to turn the view, pinch or scroll to zoom toward the pointer, and right-drag (or drag with two fingers) to move it. Double-click or double-tap anything to fly to it and turn about it there. Tap or hover over something for its distance and where it is in your sky.

Galaxy and Nearby hold the galaxy still, so you watch your horizon swing round the Sun. Horizon holds your ground level instead, so you watch the galaxy wheel round you through the night. Pick a night and a time with the sliders, or press Play.

Each Messier object sits at its distance, in its direction from the Sun. The arms are drawn from a simple model of the galaxy's spiral, so their shapes are approximate; the distances are catalogue values.
