Galaxy Searchlight · Dublin

The Milky Way seen from above, with the Sun 26,700 light-years from its centre. Your horizon is a plane through the Sun, so it cuts the galaxy's disc along a line. The half of the disc above it is lit, and as the Earth turns the beam sweeps round.

  • The beam: directions in the disc above your horizon, brighter the higher they stand
  • Your horizon, labelled with the compass point at each end
  • The highest direction in the disc right now
  • The Sun: you are here
  • Globular cluster, bright when above your horizon. They swarm round the centre, which is how Harlow Shapley found where it was in 1918
  • Open cluster: young, and close to us in the disc
  • Nebula (bright, planetary or supernova remnant)

Each Messier object sits at its distance, in its direction from the Sun; globulars far above or below the disc are shown where they lie over it. Pick a night and a time with the sliders, or press Play to watch the beam sweep round. Drag to move, pinch or scroll to zoom, and tap anything (or hover over it) for its distance and where it is in your sky.

The arms are drawn from a simple model of the galaxy's spiral, so their shapes are approximate; the distances are catalogue values.
